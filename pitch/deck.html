Where it hurts, every day

  • The language wall. Students skim an English paragraph and memorise it without grasping it, because they never put the logic into their own words.
  • Dead time and tired eyes. On a crowded bus, walking, or late at night, typing questions or bilingual notes on a cramped phone keyboard is impractical. Typing Indic scripts is slower still.
  • The illusion of competence. Passive reading feels like learning. The gaps show up in the exam hall, when it is too late.
  • Asking feels risky. First-generation learners hesitate to ask "basic" questions in class.
Cities we start with: Patna, Madurai, Kolkata. First-generation college students who commute daily.

The solution

Say it in your language. Learn it in English.

1

Hear it simply

"Explain this paragraph in simple Hindi." A spoken explanation of the textbook section with an everyday Indian example: a pressure cooker, a cinema-hall queue, onion prices.

2

Ask anything

"Entropy kya hai?" Answered in your language and grounded in the English paragraph on screen. Mixed-language questions work too.

3

Explain it back

The Spoken Feynman Check: you explain the concept aloud, as if teaching a friend. The app reads back the key terms it heard, then gives a score out of 10, what you got right, one gap and a hint.

4

Keep English notes

Your spoken explanation becomes 3 to 5 exam-ready English bullet points, saved next to your own words, with a re-check one week later.

Students keep their English-medium degree, but they think, ask and explain in their own language. Gaps show up the same day, not in the exam hall.
BolkeSeekho · Solution3

Why voice comes first

Voice is the product, not a feature on top

Physically

Commuters and tired learners need a zero-touch, eyes-free way to study. Tapping menus and typing Hindi, Tamil or Bengali on a phone keyboard is slow on a moving bus. Speaking is fast.

Pedagogically

The Feynman technique works by explaining a concept aloud. Saying it in your mother tongue exposes the gaps that multiple-choice buttons and copy-paste hide.

By design

After you choose a language, every step works by voice. The input box always has focus for Wispr Flow and sends itself after a pause, so you never tap Send. Every reply is spoken and also shown in large text.

Measured

The prototype counts the share of inputs that were spoken, how many commands it understood first time, and screen taps per session. These are the numbers the pilot will report.

What is built today

Working prototype: one command, no keys

Works today

  • docker compose up, then open localhost:8080. Runs fully without any API key.
  • Replies in 5 languages (Hinglish, Hindi, Tamil, Bengali, English), spoken with on-device voices.
  • 3 chapters × 3 sections (Thermodynamics, Recursion, Supply and Demand) with Indian examples, and a 23-term glossary.
  • Commands understood in Roman Hinglish, Devanagari, Tamil and Bengali script, even when switching mid-sentence.
  • Spoken Feynman Check graded offline: rubric, misconception detection, and a check for reading the book aloud.
  • Paste your own notes to have them read aloud and quizzed. With a Claude key: open questions and AI grading.
  • 133 automated tests. CI starts the Docker container and checks the main screen.

Noise, accents and mishearing

  • Key terms are read back before grading: "Not sure about: 'adiabetic', did you mean adiabatic?" The student answers yes, or fixes the word by voice.
  • Fuzzy matching for near-misses (entrophy, bass case, equilibirum) and for English terms written in Devanagari, Tamil or Bengali script.
  • Unclear words can earn marks but never cost marks.
  • Negation is understood: "energy cannot be created" is not flagged as the misconception "energy can be created".
  • Uncertain speech is confirmed first: "Did you say …?" Filler and noise get "please say it again, a little closer to the mic".
  • Graceful degradation: if the AI does not answer, the offline engine takes over and says so.

Main risk

Mishearing that turns into wrong teaching

The risk

Wispr Flow handles Hindi-English mixing (Hinglish) today. For Tamil and Bengali it transcribes one language per dictation, so English terms like "entropy" inside a Tamil sentence are the most likely to be misheard.

A misheard term could fail a correct explanation, or the AI could explain the wrong concept, and students would stop trusting the app.

What we do about it (built into the prototype)

  • Launch Hinglish-first, and set each student's language at onboarding.
  • Match transcripts against each chapter's glossary in every script. For example, energy written as एनर्जी, எனர்ஜி or এনার্জি is still matched.
  • Read the key terms back before grading: "I heard entropy and closed system, right?"
  • Never deduct marks for low-confidence words.
  • Ground every explanation in the source paragraph shown on screen.
  • Detect misconceptions only where there is no negation, so a correct statement is never penalised.
  • Faculty spot-check AI grades during the pilot.
Tamil and Bengali code-switching follows as Wispr Flow extends mixed-language support beyond Hindi (announced for the next 12 months).

Four measurements, decided before the pilot starts

Learning gain

In each pilot class, half the students use BolkeSeekho for two chapters and half study as usual. Both halves take the same concept test two weeks later.

Retention

Share of concepts passed in a Feynman Check that still score 7/10 or more when re-checked one week later. The app schedules the re-check.

80% · 90%

Voice-first proof

80% of study sessions finished without touching the screen, and 90% of commands understood the first time. The prototype already measures both.

↓ backlogs

Real outcomes

Pass rates and backlogs in the pilot subjects, compared with the previous batch at the same colleges.

Launch plan

Write 30 faculty-reviewed chapter packs for the first-year subjects every engineering and science student takes (Engineering Physics, Chemistry, Programming in C), plus BCom Economics. Hinglish first.

Jan – May 2027 · Pilot

1,000 Hinglish-speaking students in 2 or 3 Patna colleges for one semester, free for students. Faculty spot-check grades. We report the four measurements.

Jul – Dec 2027 · Expand

Madurai (Tamil) and Kolkata (Bengali), as Wispr Flow's mixed-language support grows. Goal: 50,000 learners by the end of year one.

How we reach the first users

  • A faculty champion in each partner college, who also spot-checks grades.
  • One student ambassador per section, demos at first-year orientation, and class WhatsApp groups.
  • Launch two weeks before mid-semester exams, when students need revision most.

How it grows and pays for itself

  • Notes written from the student's own words are easy to share on WhatsApp, and each one is an invitation.
  • Class streaks and a faculty dashboard bring in whole first-year batches through their college.
  • Free for students. Colleges pay about ₹200 per student per semester for AI grading and the dashboard. The AI cost on Claude Haiku 5.5 is about ₹25 per student per semester.
